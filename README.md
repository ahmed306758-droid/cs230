Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client?

The Gaming Room was the client seeking to expand its existing game, Draw It or Lose It. The goal was to broaden access by developing a version that could be played across multiple platforms rather than being limited to Android devices. The client wanted to enhance their reach and provide a seamless gaming experience for users regardless of their device or operating system.
What type of software did they want you to design?

The Gaming Room requested a cross-platform version of Draw It or Lose It, modeled after the existing Android application. They needed assistance with setting up the development environment and optimizing the process for efficiency. The new system had to ensure that each game and team name was unique, only one game instance could exist in memory at a time, and that multiple teams—or even individual teams—could participate simultaneously. Each team would also have multiple players assigned, requiring a flexible and scalable design.
What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?

According to my instructor’s feedback, I performed exceptionally well in the evaluation section of the documentation. I was able to clearly articulate and analyze different architectural options and provide strong reasoning for the recommendations made. This demonstrated my ability to evaluate technical solutions effectively and communicate them clearly.
What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?
I found that the structured and organized format of the design document was incredibly beneficial. As someone who tends to over-document, following a clear and concise template helped me stay focused on meeting the specific requirements without overcomplicating the content. The document’s structure guided my thought process and ensured that I maintained clarity while including all essential details.

If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I were to revise any section, I would include pseudocode within the template. Although I once found pseudocode challenging, I’ve grown to appreciate its value in outlining logic and flow for larger projects. Including pseudocode would help provide a clearer picture of how the system should function, improving team understanding and coordination. It would also allow developers to identify which skills and resources are needed to meet the client’s expectations more effectively.

How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? 
Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?

Understanding and translating user needs into technical design is an area where I excel, and this project was no exception. Acting as a bridge between the client’s vision and the technical implementation is essential for success. It’s important to listen carefully to what the client wants and then transform those requirements into functional software specifications that developers can follow. When user needs are misunderstood or ignored, the result is often dissatisfaction and wasted effort. Effective communication and accurate interpretation ensure that both clients and developers achieve their goals, leading to a product that meets expectations and adds real value.

How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?

My approach to software design is rooted in research and analysis. I dedicate time to studying documentation, reviewing best practices, and understanding various design methodologies before beginning development. This prevents costly mistakes that can occur when diving in without proper preparation. I prefer to break the project into smaller, manageable sections—an approach aligned with Agile methodologies—which allows for iterative improvements and flexibility. I’ve found that being detailed and methodical in the planning and design phases ultimately makes development and testing more efficient and effective.
